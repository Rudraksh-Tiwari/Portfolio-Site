Three design directions

Same content, same four projects, same numbers. What changes is the visual language: what leads, how dense it is, and what a recruiter's eye lands on first. Pick one and I will build the whole site in it.

Direction A  /  Instrument

Numbers lead. No serif anywhere. Dark, dense, mono-forward, closer to a spec sheet or an instrument panel than a portfolio. Metrics get their own bordered grid at the top, projects become scannable rows. Highest information density of the three, and the most literally on-message for someone whose pitch is correctness under concurrency. Risk: it reads engineered rather than crafted, and there is nowhere for personality to sit.

Direction B  /  Dispatch

Light, printed, and almost nobody does it. Every developer portfolio a recruiter opens that week is dark. This one is paper. Serif display, wide margins, a marginal column carrying the number while the prose carries the story. Projects read as short articles rather than cards. Risk: it is the least "engineer" of the three, and the live demos have to be redrawn for a light ground.

Direction C  /  Runtime

The simulation is the page. Your four live demos stop being illustrations next to text and become the primary surface: full-bleed, running, with the type reduced to a caption over them. Mono throughout, the machine's own voice. This is the version a recruiter screenshots. Risk: it is the most work to build, and if someone skims with motion reduced it has the least to fall back on.
